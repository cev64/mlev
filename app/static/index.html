      Does the model actually work?

      Walk-forward validation: train on every season before season N, predict season N, roll forward. Nothing the model sees during training comes from the season it is being scored on, so these numbers are what it would really have done.

      Predict upcoming fixtures

      Trains on everything played so far, then predicts what has not been played yet. Every number is a probability or a distribution — never a single pick.

        Every side, as a percentage

        Both sides of every market, with the fair price the model implies. Type a book's price next to any side and you get the edge, the expected value per $100, and the Kelly stake.

        How to read this

        Model % is the chance the model gives that side. Where a push is possible the percentage shown is the chance it wins outright, and the push is listed separately — a −3 spread that lands on 3 returns your stake rather than losing it.

        Fair is the price with no margin at all: what a book taking zero cut would post. Any real price will be worse than this. The question is by how much.

        Edge compares the model against the price you typed, after removing the push. Enter the other side's price too and you also get the de-vigged comparison, which strips the book's margin and is the honest version.

        EV is expected profit on your stake. It is the number that decides whether a price is worth taking — a 3-point edge on a longshot is worth far more than 3 points on a heavy favourite.

        Kelly is an upper bound on what a bankroll can justify, assuming the model's probability is exactly right. It never is, so most people bet a fraction of it.

        Before you act on any of this: check the Backtest tab. The model beats the base rate on NFL moneylines and EPL match results; it does not beat it on EPL totals or both-teams-to-score. A number being shown here does not mean it is worth betting.
